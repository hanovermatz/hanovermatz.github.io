Evolutionary Immunology

I am an immunologist studying the fundamental principles of adaptive immunity. My work synthesizes molecular biology, immunology, and comparative evolutionary approaches to understand how immune responses are generated, selected, and refined over time.

The adaptive immune system is the product of millions of years of evolution in jawed vertebrates. Through the coordination of B cells, T cells, and antigen-presenting cells, it creates highly specific, durable protection against infection.

Research Interests

  • B cells and antibodies
  • Evolution of adaptive immunity
  • Improving vaccine design

Research Highlights

Mechanisms of B cell biology

B cells produce antibodies that defend the host from infectious pathogens. Through the germinal center response, B cells are able to undergo mutation, selection, and affinity maturation, enabling the immune system to generate highly specific and protective responses.

Evolution of B cell selection

Many vertebrate models can be studied to understand the evolution of the immune system. Sharks are the oldest living organisms with an immune system like humans: B cells, T cells, and specialized immune tissues that provide a window into the origins of adaptive immunity.

Vaccine design and combating disease

What we learn from human, mouse, and comparative immunology studies informs how we can modulate vaccines to better induce protective and durable immunity. Many pathogens are difficult targets, and understanding how immune selection shapes response quality is essential to improving prevention.
